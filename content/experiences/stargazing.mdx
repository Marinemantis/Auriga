On a clear night in the high valleys of Gilgit-Baltistan, the sky is so full of stars that it is hard to pick out the constellations. The reasons are simple: altitude, dry air and distance. Many of the best places sit at 3,000 to 4,000 metres, far above the haze of the plains, and hours from the nearest town of any size.

## Where to go

### Deosai Plains

The great high plateau between Skardu and Astore, at around 4,000 metres, is our favourite place for the night sky. There are no villages, no roads lit at night and almost nothing on the horizon. Our journey [Where Mountains Meet the Stars](/tours/mountains-meet-stars) includes a night at a camp on the plateau.

### Fairy Meadows

From the meadow beneath Nanga Parbat, the stars appear above one of the highest mountain walls on Earth. On a moonlit night the face of the mountain glows; on a moonless one, the Milky Way rises above it.

### Khukush Lake and the Ghizer valley

Remote alpine lakes such as Khukush, reached on our [Khukush Lake & Phunder](/tours/khukush-lake-phunder) journey, give you still water, dark skies and complete silence.

### The Katpana desert

Just outside Skardu, the [Katpana cold desert](/destinations/skardu/katpana-desert) is the easiest dark-sky spot to reach, with dunes framed by mountains.

## When to go

The high camps are open from roughly May to October, and that is the season for stargazing in the mountains. Plan around the **new moon**, when the sky is darkest, and look for the bright core of the Milky Way in the evening sky in summer. Autumn brings crisp, very clear nights, though colder ones.

## Making the most of it

- Give your eyes twenty minutes in the dark, and use a red light rather than a white torch.
- Bring far more warm clothing than you think you need. High camps can drop to freezing at night even in July.
- For photographs, a tripod and a wide lens make a huge difference.
