The Shandur Pass is a wide, high grassland at 3,734 metres, ringed by glaciated peaks, on the border between Chitral and Gilgit-Baltistan. For most of the year, it is empty. Every July, it becomes the setting for one of the most extraordinary sporting events on Earth.

## The polo

Teams from **Gilgit** and **Chitral** meet on the world's highest polo ground, continuing a rivalry that stretches back centuries. This is free-style mountain polo: few rules, no helmets, and horsemanship at its rawest and most spectacular. The final match draws the loudest crowd.

## The festival

Around the matches, the plateau fills with tents, music, folk dancing and food stalls, and thousands of people come from across the north and the rest of Pakistan. At night, the camps glow under a huge, clear sky.

## When it happens

Usually over around three days in the first part of July. Dates are announced each year.

## Getting there

From the Chitral side, the road climbs via Mastuj. From Gilgit, it follows the Gilgit River west through Gupis and [Phander](/destinations/ghizer/phander-lake). Both are long drives on mountain roads; allow plenty of time, especially around the festival.

## Outside the festival

Even without the polo, Shandur is extraordinary: a plateau of low light, wildflowers, a quiet lake and enormous sky. Our [Wilderness of Chitral & Phunder](/tours/chitral-phunder) journey crosses Shandur from Chitral to Ghizer.
