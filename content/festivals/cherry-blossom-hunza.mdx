Spring arrives in Hunza in a rush. After a long, brown winter, the apricot trees flower first, then the cherries, and within days the terraced orchards of the valley are covered in blossom, with the snow still bright on Rakaposhi and Ultar above. It is one of the most photographed sights in Pakistan, and one of the most fleeting.

## When it happens

Blossom season usually runs from late March through April. **Apricot blossom** comes first, often in late March on the lower valley floor, and **cherry blossom** follows in April. Because Hunza climbs steadily from south to north, the bloom moves up the valley: central Hunza flowers first, and the higher villages of Gojal follow a week or two later. Each orchard is at its peak for only a week or so, and a cold spell can shift everything by days.

## Where to see it

- **Karimabad and Altit**, where orchards surround the old forts, with Rakaposhi across the valley.
- **The lanes of Altit village** and the Royal Garden below Altit Fort.
- **Gulmit and the villages of Upper Hunza**, later in the season.
- **Viewpoints above Karimabad**, such as Eagle's Nest at Duikar, for the wider view over a valley in flower.

## Planning a spring journey

Because the timing moves from year to year, a blossom journey works best with some flexibility, and with time in more than one part of the valley, so you can follow the bloom. Days are mild, in the mid-teens in central Hunza in April, but nights are cold, so bring warm layers. Spring is also quieter than summer, which makes the villages and forts especially peaceful.
