The Karakoram Highway, or **KKH**, is the road to the north. It leaves the plains near Islamabad, follows the Indus deep into the mountains, and climbs through Gilgit and Hunza to the **[Khunjerab Pass](/destinations/hunza/khunjerab-pass)**, around 4,700 metres, on the border with China. It is often called the eighth wonder of the world, and for once the phrase is deserved.

## History

The highway was built jointly by Pakistan and China, through earthquake country, landslide zones and some of the deepest gorges on Earth, and completed in 1979. Hundreds of workers died building it. A Chinese cemetery near Gilgit remembers many of them.

## The route

- **Hazara and Besham**: the road leaves the motorway and enters the hills.
- **The Indus gorge**: through Dasu and Chilas, between bare mountain walls far above the river.
- **Raikot Bridge**: the turn for [Fairy Meadows](/destinations/fairy-meadows) and Nanga Parbat.
- **Gilgit**: the region's capital, near the meeting point of three great ranges.
- **Hunza**: past Rakaposhi, Karimabad and the forts.
- **Upper Hunza**: [Attabad Lake](/destinations/hunza/attabad-lake), Gulmit, the Hussaini bridge and the [Passu Cones](/destinations/hunza/passu-cones).
- **Sost and the Khunjerab Pass**: the high plateau at the Chinese border.

## When to travel

The highway is open all year to Upper Hunza. The Khunjerab Pass section is open from spring to late autumn and closes in winter. Landslides can close sections at any time, most often in spring and during the monsoon.

## Driving the KKH

The highway is long, and mountain driving is slow. Allow plenty of time, travel by daylight, and use an experienced local driver. The best parts are worth lingering over.
