There is no single best time to visit Gilgit-Baltistan. It depends on what you want to see, how high you want to go, and how much you mind crowds.

## Spring: March to May

Blossom arrives first in the lower valleys, from late March, and spreads upwards through April. Apricot and cherry orchards turn white and pink beneath snowy peaks, especially in [Hunza](/pakistan/festivals/cherry-blossom-hunza). Days are mild; nights are cold. Most high passes are still closed.

## Summer: June to August

Peak season. High passes open, glaciers glisten, and wildflower meadows peak on Deosai, Fairy Meadows and Minimarg. The valleys are warm, the heights cool. This is the season for treks, [Deosai](/destinations/skardu/deosai-plains) and the [Shandur Polo Festival](/pakistan/festivals/shandur-polo-festival). It is also the busiest time, with domestic tourism at its height around Eid and the school holidays.

## Autumn: September to November

Golden light floods the valleys as poplars and orchards turn amber and crimson, usually peaking in October. Skies are clear, trails remain open into October, and crowds thin. For many, this is the connoisseur's choice.

## Winter: December to February

High passes close under snow, and most treks and camps shut. Hunza, Gilgit and Skardu remain accessible, quiet and beautiful, though flights are less reliable. See [Gilgit-Baltistan in winter](/travel/winter).

## By destination

- **Hunza**: April and October are our favourites. See [the best time to visit Hunza](/destinations/hunza/best-time-to-visit).
- **Skardu**: April to October; July to August for Deosai. See [Skardu weather](/destinations/skardu/weather).
- **Fairy Meadows**: roughly May to October. See [the best time to visit Fairy Meadows](/destinations/fairy-meadows/best-time-to-visit).
- **Treks**: June to September for most high routes.
