Gilgit-Baltistan is a region of many valleys, languages and peoples, and its food varies from one valley to the next. What unites it is simplicity: food grown nearby, cooked slowly, and stored carefully for winter.

## Hunza

The apricot is everything in Hunza: eaten fresh, dried on the rooftops, pressed for oil. Try **chapshoro**, a thin bread filled with spiced meat, **diram fitti**, a bread of sprouted wheat, and walnut cake in the cafés of Karimabad. See our [Hunza food guide](/destinations/hunza/food).

## Baltistan

Balti cooking has old links with Ladakh and Tibet. Try **mamtu** dumplings, **balay** noodle soup, and salty **butter tea**, drunk through the winter. See [eating in Skardu](/destinations/skardu/restaurants).

## Gilgit

Gilgit's bazaars bring together food from across the region and the rest of Pakistan: grilled meat, breads, dals and fresh fruit. See [eating in Gilgit](/destinations/gilgit/restaurants).

## Fruit through the year

Cherries in early summer, then apricots and mulberries, and later apples, grapes and walnuts. Eating fruit straight from the orchards is one of the great pleasures of the north.
