On the northern side of the Baltoro Glacier, between Paiju and Urdukas, a group of granite spires rises above the ice. These are the **Trango Towers**. Their walls are among the tallest and steepest on Earth, and they have drawn the world's best big-wall climbers for half a century.

## The towers

- **Great Trango Tower**, around 6,286 metres, whose east face drops well over a kilometre in near-vertical granite.
- **Trango Tower**, often called the **Nameless Tower**, around 6,239 metres, a slender finger of rock.
- **Trango Monk** and **Trango Pulpit**, smaller towers around them.

## Climbing history

The Nameless Tower was first climbed in **1976** by a British team, **Joe Brown, Mo Anthoine, Martin Boysen and Malcolm Howells**. Since then, the towers have seen some of the hardest big-wall climbs ever made, often over many days on the wall.

## Seeing the Trango Towers

You do not need to climb to see them. On the trek up the Baltoro Glacier towards [Concordia](/trekking/concordia) and [K2 Base Camp](/trekking/k2-base-camp), the towers rise above the glacier for much of a day's walk, most dramatically around Paiju and Urdukas.
