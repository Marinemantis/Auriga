Rakaposhi stands on the Nagar side of the Hunza River, rising to 7,788 metres. What makes it extraordinary is not its height but its scale: it climbs almost directly from the valley floor, around 5,800 metres above the river, in a single great sweep of snow and ice. Locals also call it **Dumani**, the "mother of mists".

## History

Rakaposhi was first climbed in **1958** by a British-Pakistani expedition, with **Mike Banks and Tom Patey** reaching the summit.

## Where to see it

- **[Karimabad](/destinations/hunza/karimabad)** and **[Eagle's Nest](/destinations/hunza/eagles-nest)**: Rakaposhi fills the view to the south.
- **The Rakaposhi viewpoint** on the Karakoram Highway, near Ghulmet, where the mountain rises directly above the road.
- **[Nagar](/destinations/nagar)**, on the mountain's own side of the river.
- **[Rakaposhi Base Camp](/trekking/rakaposhi-base-camp)**, above Minapin, facing its ice walls.
