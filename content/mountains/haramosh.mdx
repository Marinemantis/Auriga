Haramosh, at 7,409 metres, rises above the Indus where the river cuts through its deep gorge between Gilgit and Skardu. From the road it is mostly hidden. From the Haramosh Valley, above, its great ice walls fill the sky.

## History

Haramosh is remembered for a tragic Oxford University expedition in **1957**, when an accident high on the mountain led to a long and desperate struggle for survival. The story was later told in the book *The Last Blue Mountain*. The peak was first climbed the following year, in **1958**, by an Austrian expedition.

## Seeing Haramosh

The best way to see Haramosh is the trek to **[Kutwal Lake](/trekking/kutwal-lake)**, which climbs from the Indus through the villages of the Haramosh Valley to the lake beneath the mountain's north side. It is one of the quietest and most beautiful treks in Gilgit-Baltistan.
