Nanga Parbat means "naked mountain" in Urdu, perhaps because its steep faces hold little snow. Locally it is also called **Diamer**, "king of mountains". At 8,126 metres it is the ninth highest mountain in the world, and it marks the western end of the Himalaya, where the Indus bends around it.

## Two great faces

- **The Raikot Face**, on the north, rises above **[Fairy Meadows](/destinations/fairy-meadows)**. A trail leads from the meadow to its [base camp](/trekking/nanga-parbat-base-camp).
- **The Rupal Face**, on the south, rises around 4,600 metres above the Rupal Valley in Astore. It is the highest mountain wall on Earth; see our guide to the [Rupal Face](/trekking/rupal-face).

## History

Nanga Parbat was one of the first 8,000-metre peaks to be attempted, and one of the most deadly. Several German expeditions in the 1930s ended in tragedy, giving it the name **Killer Mountain**. In 1953, the Austrian climber **Hermann Buhl** reached the summit alone, in one of the most famous climbs in mountaineering history. In 1970, Reinhold and Günther Messner climbed the Rupal Face; Günther died on the descent.

## Where to see it

- **[Fairy Meadows](/destinations/fairy-meadows)**: the classic view of the Raikot Face across the meadow.
- **The Rupal Valley**, in [Astore](/destinations/astore): the overwhelming south face.
- **The Karakoram Highway**, near Raikot Bridge, and from viewpoints near the confluence of the Indus and Gilgit rivers.
- **The Deosai Plains**, on clear days, far to the west.
