K2 rises on the border between Pakistan and China, at the head of the Godwin-Austen Glacier in the central Karakoram. At 8,611 metres, it is only around 240 metres lower than Everest, and it is far harder to climb.

## The name

In 1856, the surveyor **Thomas Montgomerie** of the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India recorded the peaks of the Karakoram as K1, K2, K3 and so on. Most later received local names; K2, hidden from any inhabited valley, did not, and its survey label became its name.

## The Savage Mountain

K2's steep faces, exposed ridges and fierce, unpredictable weather have earned it the name **Savage Mountain**. A fatal accident in 1953 led to the phrase. For many years, it had one of the highest fatality rates of any 8,000-metre peak.

## History

- **1954**: an Italian expedition led by Ardito Desio made the first ascent, with **Lino Lacedelli** and **Achille Compagnoni** reaching the summit.
- **2021**: a team of Nepali climbers made the first winter ascent, the last great prize among the 8,000-metre peaks.

## Seeing K2

There are no roads to K2. The way to see it is on foot: a trek of around a week from Askole up the Baltoro Glacier to **[Concordia](/trekking/concordia)**, where K2 appears at the head of the valley, and on to **[K2 Base Camp](/trekking/k2-base-camp)**.
