Long before Islam reached the mountains, the valleys of the north lay on the routes that carried Buddhism from South Asia to Central Asia and China. Monks, pilgrims and traders passed through, and they left their mark on the rocks. The **Kargah Buddha** is the finest example near Gilgit.

## The carving

High on a cliff face in the Kargah valley, a standing Buddha is carved in outline into the rock, several metres tall. It is generally dated to around the **7th century**. The figure stands with one hand raised, gazing over the stream below.

## Gilgit's Buddhist past

Not far away, the remains of a Buddhist stupa and monastery have been found, and in the early twentieth century a hoard of ancient Buddhist manuscripts, the **Gilgit Manuscripts**, was discovered near Gilgit. They are among the oldest surviving Buddhist manuscripts in the world.

Across the north, carvings of the Buddha and of stupas appear on rocks, from the Indus near Chilas to the [Manthal Buddha rock](/destinations/skardu/satpara-lake) near Skardu.

## Visiting

The Kargah Buddha is a short drive west of Gilgit. A path leads to a viewpoint beneath the cliff. Afternoon light shows the carving best.
