Gilgit is where the valleys of the north come to trade, and its food shows it. You will find dishes from Hunza, Nagar and Baltistan alongside the grilled meats, curries and breads of the rest of Pakistan.

## What to eat

- **Chapshoro**: the meat-filled bread of the mountains.
- **Grilled meat and fresh bread**, from roadside grills.
- **Dals and vegetable dishes**, simple and filling.
- **Dried fruit and nuts**: apricots, mulberries, walnuts and almonds from across the north, sold by weight in the bazaar.
- **Fresh fruit** in summer and autumn: cherries, apricots, grapes and apples.

## Where to eat

Gilgit has many simple local restaurants, and the better hotels serve reliable Pakistani and continental meals. For guests, we choose places we know well, and arrange special meals in advance.

## In the bazaar

Walking the bazaar is one of the best ways to taste Gilgit. Look for dried apricots and mulberries, apricot kernels and oil, and fresh bread from the tandoor.

## On our journeys

Most guests spend a night in Gilgit at the start or end of a journey. We plan where you eat as carefully as where you stay. See our [Gilgit-Baltistan food guide](/travel/food-guide) for the rest of the north.
