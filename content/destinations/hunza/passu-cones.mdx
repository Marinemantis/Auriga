North of Attabad Lake, the Karakoram Highway enters Gojal, the upper part of the Hunza valley, and the landscape changes. The orchards thin out, the valley widens into gravel riverbeds, and above the village of Passu a wall of sharp, tooth-like spires appears on the far side of the river. These are the Passu Cones, sometimes called the Passu Cathedral, and their highest point is Tupopdan, at roughly 6,100 metres.

## Where to see them

The Cones are visible from a long stretch of the Karakoram Highway around Passu and Hussaini, and you don't need to walk far to see them well. A few of our favourite angles:

- **The roadside viewpoints near Passu**, where the highway runs straight towards the Cones along the river.
- **The Hussaini Suspension Bridge**, a long, gap-toothed footbridge over the Hunza River with the Cones behind it. Crossing it is not for everyone, but the view from the bank is just as good.
- **The approach to Borith Lake**, a small lake above Hussaini with wide views across the valley.

## When to go and the best light

The Cones are spectacular in every season, but the classic photographs are taken in clear weather from late spring to autumn. Early morning and late afternoon give the best texture on the rock, and in October the poplars along the river turn gold beneath them. In winter, Upper Hunza is very cold and some guesthouses close.

## Getting there

Passu is roughly an hour and a half north of Karimabad on the Karakoram Highway, passing Attabad Lake and the tunnels along its shore. It is easily visited as a day trip from central Hunza, but staying a night in Gojal gives you both the evening and morning light.

## What else to see nearby

- **Attabad Lake**, the turquoise lake formed by a landslide in 2010.
- **Borith Lake** and the **Hussaini Suspension Bridge**.
- **Passu and Batura glaciers**, both reachable on walks from the valley.
- **Gulmit**, a historic village and the old summer capital of the Mirs of Hunza.
- **Khunjerab Pass** on the Chinese border, a few hours further north.
