Driving north from Karimabad, the Karakoram Highway enters a series of tunnels and emerges beside a lake of startling turquoise, stretching up the valley between bare rock walls. Attabad Lake looks timeless, but it is barely more than a decade old.

## How the lake was formed

In January 2010, a massive landslide fell from the mountainside above the village of Attabad and dammed the Hunza River. Over the following months, the water rose behind the debris, flooding villages upstream and submerging a long section of the Karakoram Highway. For several years, the only way through was by boat. Tunnels were later built along the shore to carry the highway around the lake.

It is worth remembering this history when you visit. For the people of the surrounding villages, the lake was a catastrophe before it became an attraction.

## What to do

- **Take a boat out** onto the lake. It is the best way to appreciate its scale and colour. Our journeys include a private boat crossing.
- **Stop at the viewpoints** along the highway and the tunnels.
- **Stay by the water** at one of the lakeside places to stay, a good base between central and Upper Hunza.

## When to visit

The lake is accessible year round, as the highway stays open, but its colour is at its best on clear days in spring and autumn. In deep winter, parts of the lake can freeze.

## Nearby

North of the lake lie Gulmit, the Hussaini Suspension Bridge, Borith Lake and the [Passu Cones](/destinations/hunza/passu-cones). Karimabad is a short drive south.
