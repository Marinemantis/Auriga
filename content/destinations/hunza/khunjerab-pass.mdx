North of Passu, the Karakoram Highway leaves the last villages of Upper Hunza behind and climbs through a long, empty valley towards the border. At the top, at around 4,700 metres, a wide, windswept plateau marks the frontier between Pakistan and China. It is one of the highest paved border crossings on Earth.

## The drive up

The road climbs from Sost, the last town on the Pakistani side, through **Khunjerab National Park**, a high-altitude reserve created to protect the wildlife of the region. With luck you may see ibex, marmots or yaks along the way; the park is also home to the rare Marco Polo sheep and the snow leopard, though both are rarely seen. The scenery grows wilder and barer as the road climbs.

## At the top

The pass is a broad, open plateau ringed by peaks, often cold and windy even in summer. The border gate and its monuments are the focus for most visitors, and there is a real sense of standing at the edge of two worlds. Most people stay for a short while and then descend.

## When to go

The border crossing is usually open from late spring to late autumn and closed in winter, when snow blocks the road. Even in July and August, temperatures at the top can be close to freezing, so bring warm clothing.

## Altitude

Because the road climbs so quickly, Khunjerab is the highest point many travellers have ever reached. Take it slowly at the top, drink water, and descend if you feel unwell. Spending a night or two in Hunza beforehand helps.

## Planning a visit

Khunjerab is a full day trip from central Hunza, or an easy half day from Upper Hunza, so we often plan a night in Gulmit or Passu beforehand. It is included on [The Realm of Fairies](/tours/realm-of-fairies) and [The Last Paradise](/tours/last-paradise).
