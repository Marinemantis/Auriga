Above the rooftops of Karimabad, framed by the peaks of Ultar and Rakaposhi across the valley, stands Baltit Fort. For centuries it was the seat of the Mirs, the hereditary rulers of Hunza, and it is the building that best tells the story of the valley.

## A short history

Parts of the fort are thought to date back around 700 years, though it was rebuilt and extended many times. Its design shows strong Tibetan and Balti influence, a reminder of the old links between Hunza and Baltistan. The Mirs lived here until the mid-twentieth century, when the family moved to a new palace lower down, and the fort was gradually abandoned.

In the 1990s it was carefully restored by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture and reopened as a museum in 1996. The restoration is regarded as one of the finest heritage projects in the region, and it helped spark the wider revival of old Karimabad.

## Visiting the fort

Visits are by guided tour. You pass through the old reception rooms, kitchens and storerooms, look down through the trapdoors that once served as the only entrances, and finish on the roof, where the view takes in the whole of central Hunza. Allow an hour or so, and go early or late in the day when the light on the valley is softest.

## Altit Fort

A short drive away, Altit Fort is the older of the two, set on a cliff high above the Hunza River. It too has been restored and is surrounded by the Royal Garden and the traditional village of Altit. Seeing both on the same day gives a fuller picture of Hunza's past.

## Around Karimabad

The walk up to Baltit takes you through the lanes and bazaar of Karimabad, with its craft shops, carpets, Hunza caps and dried apricots. Nearby, the Eagle's Nest viewpoint above Duikar is the place for sunrise and sunset over the valley.
