Shimshal is the most remote permanent settlement in Hunza. Until a road was built along the river gorge east of Passu, it was reached only on foot, and it still feels like a world apart: a wide, high valley of fields and stone houses ringed by huge peaks, with the herders' summer pastures of the Shimshal Pamir beyond.

## Getting there

The road leaves the Karakoram Highway near Passu and follows the narrow gorge of the Shimshal river for hours, clinging to cliffs and crossing landslides. It is one of the most dramatic drives in the Karakoram, and it should be done in a sturdy jeep with an experienced driver.

## The village and its people

Shimshal is famous for its climbers. For a village of its size, it has produced an extraordinary number of Pakistan's leading mountaineers, and the tradition of high-altitude guiding runs deep here. Life follows the rhythm of the seasons, with families taking yaks, sheep and goats up to the high pastures in summer.

## Treks

Shimshal is the starting point for treks to the **Shimshal Pamir**, the high grazing lands near the Chinese border, and over the **Shimshal Pass**. These are serious multi-day treks that need careful planning and local support.

## Shimshal Kuch

When the herders bring their animals back down from the high pastures, the village celebrates with music and dancing. It is one of the most authentic festivals in the north. See our [festivals of Pakistan](/pakistan/festivals) page.

## Planning a visit

Shimshal is best visited with time to spare, at least a night or two in the valley. We can build it into a tailor made journey through Upper Hunza.
