Hunza changes completely through the year. The right time to visit depends on what you want to see: blossom, glaciers, autumn colour or snow. Here is how the seasons compare. For month by month temperatures, see our [Hunza weather guide](/destinations/hunza/weather).

## Spring: late March to May

Spring is blossom season. Apricot trees flower first, then cherry, and for a few weeks the terraced orchards of Karimabad and Altit turn white and pink beneath snow-covered peaks. Days are mild and clear; nights are still cold. See [cherry blossom in Hunza](/pakistan/festivals/cherry-blossom-hunza).

## Summer: June to August

Summer is the most reliable season for the high country. The Khunjerab Pass is open, the glacier walks are clear of snow, and fruit ripens in the orchards: cherries in early summer, apricots in July. Afternoons on the valley floor are warm but rarely hot. This is also the busiest time, when domestic tourism peaks.

## Autumn: September to early November

Autumn is our favourite. The air is clear, the crowds thin, and by October the poplars and fruit trees turn gold and red across the whole valley. See [Hunza in autumn](/destinations/hunza/in-autumn).

## Winter: December to February

Winter is cold, still and quiet. Snow settles on the higher villages, the Khunjerab Pass closes, and many hotels close for the season. For travellers who want solitude and snow, it can be magical. See [Hunza in winter](/destinations/hunza/in-winter).

## Our advice

For a first journey, choose April or October. If the Khunjerab Pass or glacier walks matter most, choose June to September. We plan every [Hunza tour](/tours/hunza) around the season you choose.
