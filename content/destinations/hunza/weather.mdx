Hunza's valley floor sits roughly 700 to 1,000 metres higher than Gilgit, so it is noticeably cooler through the year: pleasant rather than hot in summer, and cold in winter. Like the rest of the Karakoram it is very dry, with clear skies for much of the year.

## Hunza weather by month

Average figures for Aliabad in central Hunza, at about 2,200 metres. Karimabad sits a little higher and is slightly cooler; Upper Hunza (Gulmit, Passu and Sost) is colder still.

## Spring: March to May

Spring starts slowly. March is still cold at night, but by early April the apricot and then the cherry trees come into flower, starting on the lower valley floor and moving up the valley over the following weeks. May is warm, green and one of the loveliest months of the year. See our guide to [cherry blossom season in Hunza](/pakistan/festivals/cherry-blossom-hunza).

## Summer: June to August

Summer days in central Hunza are warm, often in the high twenties, with cool evenings. This is the season for treks, glacier walks and the drive up to Khunjerab Pass at around 4,700 metres, where it can be close to freezing even in July. It is also the busiest time of year in the valley.

## Autumn: September to November

September is settled and clear. Through October the poplars and orchards turn gold and red, and the light is extraordinary, which makes this the favourite season of many photographers. By November the nights are below freezing and the high passes begin to close.

## Winter: December to February

Winter in Hunza is cold, quiet and very beautiful. Highs on the valley floor are just above freezing and nights fall well below zero. Many guesthouses close for the season, and higher areas, including Khunjerab Pass, can be cut off by snow. Winter journeys are possible but need careful planning.

## How altitude changes the weather

Temperatures in the Karakoram fall by roughly six or seven degrees with every thousand metres you climb. A warm afternoon in Karimabad can be a cold one at Passu, and a freezing one at Khunjerab Pass. Always pack layers, even in summer.
