Food in Hunza follows the seasons. Through summer, orchards and terraced fields supply almost everything; through the long winter, families live on what they dried and stored. The result is a cuisine that is simple, fresh and deeply local.

## Apricots

The apricot is Hunza's signature. In summer the fruit is eaten fresh; in late summer it is laid out on rooftops to dry in the sun, and dried apricots keep families going through winter. The kernels are pressed into apricot oil, used for cooking and on the skin. You will find dried apricots, kernels and oil in every bazaar.

## Dishes to try

- **Chapshoro**: thin bread filled with spiced minced meat and cooked on a griddle. The best-known Hunza dish.
- **Diram fitti**: a traditional bread made with sprouted wheat flour, often served with apricot oil or butter.
- **Harissa**: a slow-cooked winter dish of meat and wheat.
- **Walnut cake**: a favourite in the cafés of Karimabad, best with a pot of tea.

## Fruit through the year

Cherries ripen in early summer, followed by mulberries and apricots, and then apples, grapes and walnuts in autumn. If you visit in summer, eating fruit straight from the trees is one of the pleasures of the valley.

## Where to eat

The cafés and restaurants of [Karimabad](/destinations/hunza/karimabad) serve both local dishes and simpler fare. The best meals, though, are often at home: on our journeys we arrange meals with families in the valley, so you can taste Hunza cooking where it belongs.
