Winter in Hunza runs roughly from December to February. It is cold, dry and very quiet. For travellers who want snow, solitude and clear mountain air, it can be the most memorable season of all, but it needs more planning than any other.

## What it is like

Snow settles on the higher villages and on the slopes above the valley, and the peaks stand white against hard blue skies. Days are short and cold; nights fall well below freezing. Life slows down: families gather around the stove, and the bazaar in Karimabad is half shuttered. See our [Hunza weather guide](/destinations/hunza/weather) for figures.

## What is open, and what is not

- **The Khunjerab Pass is closed** for the winter months.
- **Many hotels close.** Those that stay open vary in how well they are heated.
- **The Karakoram Highway stays open** through Hunza, though snow and rockfall can cause delays.
- **Flights** to Gilgit are more often delayed by weather than in other seasons.

## Why go

The forts are empty, the views are at their sharpest, and you see Hunza as it really lives. Frozen streams, smoke rising from village roofs, and Rakaposhi glowing at sunset: winter has a beauty no summer visitor sees.
