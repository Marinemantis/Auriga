Drive a few minutes out of Skardu towards the airport and the landscape suddenly turns to sand. Pale dunes ripple away from the Indus towards a ring of mountains, some of them snow-capped even in summer. This is the Katpana cold desert, a high-altitude desert at more than 2,000 metres, and one of the most surreal sights in the north.

## Why a desert here?

Baltistan lies in the rain shadow of the Himalaya and gets very little rain. Over thousands of years, the wind has picked up fine sediment from the wide, braided bed of the Indus and piled it into dunes. Because of the altitude, the desert is "cold": warm by day in summer, cool at night, and occasionally dusted with snow in winter, when the sight of white snow on golden sand draws photographers from across Pakistan.

## Katpana Lake

Beside the dunes lies Katpana Lake, a small, still lake that reflects the mountains on calm mornings. It is a lovely place to start or end a visit to the desert.

## Sarfaranga

On the road from Skardu towards Shigar lies a second, larger cold desert at Sarfaranga, with wide plains of sand and dunes set against the mountains of the Shigar valley. It is quieter than Katpana and easy to combine with a visit to Shigar.

## When to go and what to bring

The dunes are at their best at sunrise and sunset, when the low light picks out every ripple. Midday in summer can be hot and glaring, so bring sunglasses, a hat and water. Evenings are cool, and the clear, dry air makes the desert a good place for [stargazing](/experiences/stargazing) after dark.

## Getting there

Katpana is around a quarter of an hour from Skardu town by road. Sarfaranga is on the way to Shigar, so it fits naturally into a day exploring the Shigar valley.
