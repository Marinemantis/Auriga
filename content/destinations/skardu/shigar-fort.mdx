North of Skardu, the road crosses the Indus, passes the dunes of the Sarfaranga cold desert and enters the Shigar valley, a broad green valley of orchards and villages beneath the mountains of the Karakoram. At its heart, built against a huge boulder above the village, stands Shigar Fort, the old palace of the Rajas of Shigar. Its local name, Fong-khar, is usually translated as "the palace on the rock".

## A short history

The fort dates back around 400 years, to the seventeenth century, when the Rajas of Shigar ruled this part of Baltistan. Like the forts of Hunza, it shows the craftsmanship of the region: thick stone walls, timber frames and intricately carved wooden balconies and doorways. After the end of royal rule it fell into disrepair, until a careful restoration by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture brought it back to life as a heritage hotel and museum.

## Staying at the Shigar Fort Residence

Today the fort is the **Shigar Fort Residence**, and it is where we base our Skardu journeys. Every room is different, some in the original palace and others in restored traditional houses in the gardens, surrounded by fruit orchards and old water channels. Evenings here are quiet and unhurried, and it makes a far more memorable base than staying in Skardu town.

## Exploring the Shigar valley

- **The village and its old wooden mosques**, a short walk from the fort.
- **The Sarfaranga cold desert**, on the road from Skardu (see our [cold desert guide](/destinations/skardu/katpana-desert)).
- **The road north towards Askole**, the gateway to K2 and the Baltoro Glacier.

## Getting there

Shigar is around an hour from Skardu by road, so it works well as a base for exploring the whole region, including [Khaplu](/destinations/skardu/khaplu) and the Deosai Plains.
