Skardu's most visible landmark is easy to miss. High on a rocky ridge at the edge of town, the walls of Kharpocho Fort blend into the cliffs, and most visitors drive straight past on their way to the lakes and Deosai. Climb up, and you get the single best view of the Skardu valley: the Indus winding through its sandbanks, the dunes of Katpana, and the mountains of Baltistan in every direction.

## A short history

Kharpocho, usually translated from Balti as "king of forts", is attributed to Ali Sher Khan Anchan, the most celebrated ruler of the Maqpon dynasty, in the late sixteenth century. Under him Baltistan became a power in the western Himalaya, and the fort served as both a royal seat and a stronghold above the river. Over the following centuries it changed hands in the region's wars, and much of it is now in ruins, but the walls, gateways and an old mosque remain.

## The climb

The path starts from the edge of Skardu town and climbs steeply over rocks and loose ground. Allow around 45 minutes up and a little less down. It is not technical, but it is steep and exposed in places, so go with good shoes and plenty of water, and avoid the middle of a summer day when the rock holds the heat.

## When to go

Early morning and late afternoon give the best light over the valley and the coolest climb. The fort can be visited from spring to autumn; in winter the path can be icy.

## Nearby

- **Katpana cold desert**, the high-altitude dunes across the valley.
- **Shigar**, with its restored royal fort, now the Shigar Fort Residence, about an hour away.
- **Satpara Lake** and the road up to the **Deosai Plains**.
