Climb south from Skardu past Satpara Lake and the mountains suddenly fall away. In front of you, a vast rolling plateau of grass, streams and flowers stretches to a ring of distant peaks. This is Deosai, the "land of giants", one of the highest plateaus on Earth, at an average of around 4,100 metres.

## The plateau

Deosai is protected as a national park, created above all to save the Himalayan brown bear, whose numbers had fallen dangerously low. It is snowbound for most of the year, and for a few short summer months it turns green, crossed by clear streams and, at its height, covered in wildflowers. Golden marmots whistle from their burrows beside the track.

## Sheosar Lake

Near the western edge of the plateau lies Sheosar Lake, a wide, still lake that on calm days reflects the surrounding mountains. On clear days, the distant summit of Nanga Parbat can be seen from parts of the plateau.

## When to go

The road across Deosai is usually open from around June to September. July and August are the best months for wildflowers. Nights are cold even in midsummer, often close to freezing.

## Crossing Deosai

The road across the plateau links Skardu with Astore, and crossing it is one of the great journeys of the north. It is a long day on a rough track, and best done in a sturdy vehicle with an experienced driver.

## Camping under the stars

The best way to experience Deosai is to stay the night. ## What to bring

Warm layers, a hat and gloves, sun protection and sunglasses. The sun is strong at this altitude and the wind can be cold.
