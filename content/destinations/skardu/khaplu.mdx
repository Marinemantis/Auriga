Khaplu was once the capital of its own small kingdom in eastern Baltistan, and it still has the feel of a royal town. The houses climb a hillside above the Shyok river, threaded with water channels and shaded by apricot and walnut trees, with the old palace of its rulers at the centre.

## Khaplu Palace

Built in the nineteenth century by the Rajas of Khaplu, the palace is one of the finest examples of Balti architecture: tiers of timber and stone, carved wooden balconies and a great wooden watchtower. Like [Shigar Fort](/destinations/skardu/shigar-fort), it was carefully restored by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture and now operates as a heritage hotel and museum.

## Chaqchan Mosque

A short walk from the palace, the Chaqchan Mosque is one of the oldest mosques in Baltistan, traditionally linked with the arrival of Islam in the region in the fourteenth century. Its wooden architecture is quite unlike the mosques of the plains.

## The drive from Skardu

The road from Skardu follows the Indus and then the Shyok river east, through villages and orchards with mountains on every side. It takes around two and a half to three hours, and it is a beautiful drive in its own right.

## Beyond Khaplu

North of Khaplu, the **Hushe valley** leads towards Masherbrum and the peaks of the eastern Karakoram, and is the starting point for treks towards Masherbrum and Gondogoro La.

## When to go

Spring and autumn are the most beautiful seasons, with blossom in April and golden colour in October. Summer is warm and green.
