Baltistan has its own cuisine, shaped by long winters, short summers and its old links with Ladakh and Tibet. It is simple, warming food, made with wheat, barley, apricots, dairy and meat, and it is very different from the curries of the plains.

## Balti dishes to try

- **Mamtu**: steamed dumplings filled with minced meat and onion.
- **Balay**: a hearty soup of hand-made noodles in broth.
- **Prapu**: a traditional dish of wheat-flour pasta with apricot oil or butter.
- **Butter tea**: salty tea churned with butter, drunk through the winter.
- **Apricots**: eaten fresh in summer, dried for winter, and pressed for oil.

## Where to eat

Skardu town has plenty of simple restaurants serving Pakistani and Balti food. For a meal to remember, though, the best dining is at the heritage hotels around it.

At the **[Shigar Fort Residence](/destinations/skardu/shigar-fort)**, meals are inspired by traditional Balti cooking, with organic produce from the fort's gardens and nearby villages, served in the courtyards and orchards. Khaplu Palace offers something similar further east.
