Fairy Meadows is a place to stay, not to visit. The meadow, at around 3,300 metres, has no roads, and the people who stay the night see it at its most beautiful: at dawn and dusk, and under the stars.

## Where to stay

The meadow has wooden huts and simple lodges, and space for camps. Facilities are basic, and quality varies. In summer, it can be busy.

## Our camp

On [The Realm of Fairies](/tours/realm-of-fairies), guests stay at our curated camp at the meadow edge, in comfortable tents with proper bedding, and Nanga Parbat filling the horizon at dawn. Our team cooks, and we choose a spot for quiet and the view.

## Two nights, not one

Most visitors arrive tired in the afternoon and leave the next morning. On our journeys you stay two nights, so you see the mountain at dawn, in full daylight and under the stars, with a full day to walk towards Beyal Camp and [Nanga Parbat Base Camp](/trekking/nanga-parbat-base-camp).

## What to bring

- Warm layers, a hat and gloves: nights are cold.
- A head torch.
- Comfortable walking shoes.
- A power bank: electricity is limited.

## Stars

Far from towns, Fairy Meadows has wonderfully dark skies. See our [stargazing guide](/experiences/stargazing).

## When to go

Roughly May to October. See [the best time to visit Fairy Meadows](/destinations/fairy-meadows/best-time-to-visit).
