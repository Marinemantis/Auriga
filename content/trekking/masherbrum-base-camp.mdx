**[Masherbrum](/pakistan/mountains/masherbrum)**, at 7,821 metres, was the first peak measured by the Great Trigonometrical Survey in the Karakoram, which gave it the name **K1**. Its southern side rises above the **Hushe valley**, and a trek from Hushe village leads up the Masherbrum Glacier to its base camp.

## The route

From **[Hushe village](/trekking/hushe-valley)**, the trail heads north-west into the Masherbrum valley, climbing through summer pastures and onto the moraine of the **Masherbrum Glacier**. Camps are made in high meadows, with the mountain's great south face growing ahead. The base camp lies beneath the glacier's icefall, with views of Masherbrum and the surrounding peaks.

## Difficulty

Moderate to strenuous. The trail is not technical, but the moraine is rough and the altitude is significant, so good fitness and acclimatisation are needed.

## When to go

From June to September. July and August have the most settled weather.

## Combining treks

Hushe is the gateway to several treks: Masherbrum, **[Laila Peak](/trekking/laila-peak-base-camp)** and the Gondogoro valley, and the Charakusa valley beneath K6 and K7. With time, you can combine two or more from a single base.
