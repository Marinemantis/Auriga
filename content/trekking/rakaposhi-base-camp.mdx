**[Rakaposhi](/pakistan/mountains/rakaposhi)**, at 7,788 metres, rises almost directly from the valley floor of Hunza and Nagar, one of the greatest uninterrupted mountain slopes on Earth. The trek to its base camp from **Minapin** is one of the most scenic and accessible in northern Pakistan.

## Day 1: Minapin

A drive of two to three hours from Hunza or Gilgit brings you to **Minapin**, a village of terraced fields and orchards at around 2,000 metres, with your first views of Rakaposhi and Diran. We meet the trekking team, and spend the night in a guesthouse.

## Day 2: Minapin to Hapakun

The trail climbs through pine forest, past streams and grazing pastures, to **Hapakun**, a peaceful meadow at around 2,700 metres. Four to five hours of steady walking, with the mountain growing ever larger.

## Day 3: Hapakun to Taghaphari

The finest day. The trail opens into wide alpine terrain with uninterrupted views of the Rakaposhi Glacier, and reaches **Taghaphari**, the base camp, at around 3,500 metres, directly facing the mountain's ice walls. Five to six hours.

## Day 4: Back to Minapin

A relaxed descent of five to six hours by the same route, then onward to Hunza or Gilgit.

## Who it is for

The trail is well marked and the altitude modest, so it suits beginners, photographers and families with some walking fitness, as well as experienced trekkers looking for a short, spectacular route.
