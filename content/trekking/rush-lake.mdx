Above the village of Hopar in Nagar, the Hopar and Miar glaciers sweep down from a cirque of huge peaks. Climb beside them for several days and you reach Rush Lake, a small, often still lake at around 4,700 metres, one of the highest in the country. Above it, the summit of Rush Peak opens up a panorama of the Karakoram that few viewpoints in Pakistan can match.

## The route

The trek starts at **Hopar**, at the end of the road into Nagar's high valley, beside the Hopar (Bualtar) Glacier. From there the trail crosses the glacier and climbs through high summer pastures used by Nagar's herders, with camps along the way, before the final climb to the lake. The return follows the same route.

Most itineraries take four to six days, allowing time to acclimatise on the way up. From the lake, an optional climb to **Rush Peak** gives a wider view over the surrounding peaks, among them Spantik and Malubiting.

## Difficulty and altitude

This is a strenuous trek, not a walk. Expect long, steep days, glacier and moraine crossings, and camps at high altitude. The most important factor is acclimatisation: the trek gains a lot of height, and a slow, steady ascent with rest days makes it safer and far more enjoyable. It suits fit trekkers with some multi-day experience.

## When to go

The high route is usually free of snow from around July to September. Earlier or later in the season, snow and cold make it much harder.
