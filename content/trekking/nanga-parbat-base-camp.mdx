**[Nanga Parbat](/pakistan/mountains/nanga-parbat)**, at 8,126 metres, is the ninth highest mountain in the world and the western anchor of the Himalaya. Its north side, the **Raikot Face**, towers above **[Fairy Meadows](/destinations/fairy-meadows)**, and from the meadow a trail continues to the foot of the mountain itself.

## Getting to Fairy Meadows

From Raikot Bridge on the Karakoram Highway, a narrow jeep track climbs towards the village of Tato. The last stretch is on foot, a walk of around three hours through forest and clearings, until the meadow opens out at around 3,300 metres with Nanga Parbat filling the sky.

## Fairy Meadows to Beyal Camp

From Fairy Meadows, the trail continues gently through pine forest and along the edge of the **Raikot Glacier** to **Beyal Camp**, a small summer settlement in a clearing. The views of the mountain grow with every step.

## Beyal to Base Camp

Beyond Beyal, the path leaves the forest and climbs onto lateral moraine beside the glacier, to a viewpoint and then to the base camp, at around 3,900 metres, directly beneath the north face. The ice walls and hanging glaciers above are immense, and the sound of avalanches carries across the valley.

## Difficulty

The route is not technical, but the moraine is rough and the altitude is noticeable. It can be done as a long day from Fairy Meadows and back, or more comfortably with a night at Beyal.

## When to go

From around June to September, when the trail is clear of snow. Fairy Meadows itself is accessible from roughly May to October.
