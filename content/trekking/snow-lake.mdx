The Biafo and Hispar glaciers together form one of the longest stretches of glacier ice outside the polar regions. At their meeting point, high in the central Karakoram, lies **Snow Lake**, a vast white basin ringed by granite spires. The explorer Martin Conway gave it its name in the 1890s, thinking it looked like a frozen lake.

## The route

The traverse starts at **Askole**, the same last village used by K2 trekkers. Instead of turning east up the Baltoro, the route heads north up the **Biafo Glacier**, camping on moraine and ice, to Snow Lake. From there it climbs to the **Hispar La**, at around 5,100 metres, and descends the long **Hispar Glacier** west to the village of Hispar in **[Nagar](/destinations/nagar)**.

## What it is like

Days are long, and almost all on ice. The upper Biafo and the area around Snow Lake are crevassed and must be crossed roped together. Nights are spent on the glacier, far from any village. In return, you see a wilderness few people ever reach: granite towers, hanging glaciers, and, if you are lucky, the tracks of snow leopards.

## Who it is for

Snow Lake is for very fit, experienced trekkers who are comfortable with glacier travel, high camps and long days far from help. It needs a strong, experienced team of guides and porters, and good weather.

## When to go

July and August, when the glaciers are most passable and the weather most settled.

## Permits

The route lies in controlled areas of the Karakoram and needs a trekking permit through a registered operator. See our guide to [NOCs and permits](/travel/noc-permits).
