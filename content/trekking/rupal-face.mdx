Nanga Parbat has two great faces. The north, or Raikot, face looks down on Fairy Meadows. The south face, the **Rupal Face**, rises above the Rupal Valley in Astore, around 4,600 metres of rock and ice from the valley meadows to the summit. It is the highest mountain wall on Earth.

## The Rupal Valley

From [Astore](/destinations/astore), a road climbs to the villages of **Rupal** and **Tarishing**, at the mouth of the valley. From here, the face fills the view: impossibly high, streaked with ice and hanging glaciers.

## The trek

From Tarishing, the trail follows the valley past the Tarishing Glacier to **Herrligkoffer Base Camp**, at around 3,500 metres, named after the leader of the German expeditions that climbed the face in 1970. It is an easy to moderate walk through meadows, with the face growing above you all the way.

Beyond, the trail continues to the summer pastures of **Latobo** and, for strong trekkers, towards Shaigiri and the Mazeno Glacier, where the views of the face are even more overwhelming.

## When to go

From June to September, when the meadows are green and the trail is clear. Nights are cold.

## On our journeys

On the [Seven Day Himalayan Dream](/tours/himalayan-dream), we drive into the Rupal Valley in the afternoon, when the face catches the last sun, and stay at a lodge in Astore facing Nanga Parbat. We can add a walk to Herrligkoffer Base Camp, or a longer trek in the valley, on request.
