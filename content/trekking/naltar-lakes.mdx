Naltar is the valley that people in Gilgit escape to on hot summer days. A side valley north of the city, it climbs quickly into forests of pine, open pastures and a string of small lakes whose water shifts from deep blue to green to turquoise in the changing light. It is one of the gentlest and most beautiful days out in the region.

## Getting there

The road leaves the Karakoram Highway near Gilgit and climbs into the valley, the final section on a rough track best taken by jeep. The upper village, Naltar Bala, sits among the forests at the head of the valley, and the lakes lie beyond it.

## The lakes

The best known is **Satrangi Lake**, the "seven-coloured lake", named for the shifting colours of its water. Beyond it lies **Blue Lake**, often the most vivid of all. The lakes are reached by jeep and short walks, at around 3,000 metres, and none of the walking is difficult. Allow a full day to enjoy them without hurrying.

## A good first day in the mountains

Because the walking is easy and the altitude moderate, Naltar makes an ideal first day out after arriving in Gilgit: a chance to stretch your legs and start acclimatising before heading higher to Hunza or beyond.

## When to go

Naltar is at its best from early summer to autumn, when the lakes are open and the forest is green, turning gold in October. In winter the lakes freeze, the valley fills with snow, and Naltar becomes Gilgit-Baltistan's ski area.

## Longer treks

For experienced trekkers, the valley is also the start of the multi-day crossing of the **Naltar Pass** towards the Ishkoman valley.
